# What this is for, and what it isn't

voiceprint clones a writing voice from a small sample. That is genuinely useful — drafting faster
in your own register, getting past a blank page, writing more when writing is hard — and it is
just as obviously misusable. So the line is drawn here rather than left implied.

## Use it for

- **Your own voice.** The overwhelmingly normal case.
- **A voice you have explicit permission to write in.** A colleague you ghostwrite for, a client
  who hired you, an organization whose house style is your job.
- **Research.** The `eval` command exists so claims about this technique can be checked rather
  than believed.

## Don't use it for

- **Impersonating anyone.** Publishing under someone's name, imitating their voice to mislead
  people, or cloning a public writer from their work without their say-so.
- **Work you're submitting as your own.** Coursework, assessments, applications — anywhere the
  point of the exercise is that *you* wrote it.
- **Astroturfing.** Fake reviews, sockpuppets, manufactured accounts, synthetic grassroots.

## On AI detectors

This technique produces prose that reads as human, and `eval` can measure that with a detector if
you bring a key. That measurement exists because it's the honest way to evaluate the method, not
because evading detection is the point. The project is not positioned as a way to beat detectors,
and a pull request that makes it one will be declined.

## On your writing

Your corpus stays on your machine. The chunks derived from it are processed in **your own Modal
account** by code you can read in this repo. There is no service behind this project, so there is
nowhere for your writing to be collected, retained, or trained on by anyone but you.
